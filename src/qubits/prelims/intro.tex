\section{Intro}

In this first chapter, we introduce the basic background material we'll need for the first half of the thesis, so there's really not too much higher-level discussion to be had!
All but the final two sections of this chapter is completely standard -- one can find it all in Nielsen-Chuang~\cite{Nielsen_Chuang_2010}, or whatever your introductory quantum information textbook of choice is.
We define some basic qubit quantum information theory, almost entirely so that we can contrast it with the less popular (and more complicated) qumode case in the second half of the thesis.
We introduce the very basics of the stabilizer formalism and stabilizer codes, saving the more interesting parts for the next chapter, where we introduce dynamic stabilizer codes.
We also introduce the ZX-calculus, the first part of the chapter that can be considered in any way non-standard, but even then we just give the basics that one can find in the combination of the two standard introductory ZX textbooks~\cite{Coecke_Kissinger_2017,KissingerWetering2024Book}.
Finally, we define noise models.
We won't make loads of use of these in the qubit case, but it sets things up nicely for the second half of the thesis, where we do consider noise models more carefully.
