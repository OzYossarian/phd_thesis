\section{Intro}

Every chapter in this thesis has an intro and outro; this is where all the high-level discussion around some of the more concrete results will take place.
In this first chapter, however, we're just introducing the background material we'll need, so there's really not too much to say!
All but the final two sections of this chapter is completely standard -- one can find it all in Nielsen-Chuang, or whatever your introductory quantum information textbook of choice is.
We define some basic qubit quantum information theory, almost entirely so that we can contrast it with the less popular known (and more complicated) qumode case in the second half of thesis.
We introduce the very basics of the stabilizer formalism and stabilizer codes, saving the more interesting parts for the next chapter, where we introduce dynamic stabilizer codes.
Next, we introduce the ZX-calculus, the first part of the chapter that can be considered in any way non-standard, but even then we just give the basics that one can find in the combination of the two standard introductory ZX textbooks~\cite{Coecke_Kissinger_2017,KissingerWetering2024Book}.
Finally, we define noise models -- again, we won't make loads of use of this in the qubit case, but it sets things up nicely for the second half of the thesis, where we do consider noise models more carefully.
