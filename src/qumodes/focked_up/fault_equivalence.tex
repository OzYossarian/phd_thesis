\section{Focked up fault-equivalence}

If two ZX-diagrams are equal under the rewrite rules of the ZX-calculus, it means the linear maps they represent are equal.
But it says nothing about the behaviour of the two diagrams under noise.
The go-to example of this in the qubit ZX-calculus is the following.
Suppose we have four qubits, and we measure $Z^{\otimes 4}$ and get outcome $m$.
Letting $m^+ = \frac{1 - m}{2}$, we showed earlier (\Cref{subsec:qubit_pauli_measurements}) we can write the corresponding projector $\Pi_{mZ^{\otimes 4}}$ in the ZX-calculus as:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/qubit_z4_meas_spec}
\end{equation}

Just by spider unfusion, this diagram is equivalent to the following one, consisting of more familiar quantum circuit operations:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/qubit_z4_meas_circuit}
\end{equation}

Now, suppose we imagine noise can occur.
Specifically, consider a very simple noise model, where we suppose a single $Z$ gate can occur on any edge of a given diagram.
In the first diagram, no matter where this single $Z$ error occurs, it will be equivalent to a single $Z$ error on an output edge.
For example:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/qubit_z4_meas_spec_z_error_in}
    ~~=~~
    \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/qubit_z4_meas_spec_z_error_internal}
    ~~=~~
    \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/qubit_z4_meas_spec_z_error_out}
\end{equation}

However, in the second diagram, there's one edge where, should a $Z$ error occur, it's equivalent to two errors on output edges:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/qubit_z4_meas_circuit_z_error_bad_1}
    ~~=~~
    \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/qubit_z4_meas_circuit_z_error_bad_4}
\end{equation}

In general, this is the sort of thing we want to avoid when trying to implement a given linear map.
Very loosely speaking, if a diagram $D_1$ is equal to a diagram $D_2$, and there are no edges in $D_2$ where errors can spread like in the example above, then we say the rewrite $D_1 \mapsto D_2$ is \textit{fault-accounting}.
If the rewrite $D_2 \mapsto D_1$ is also fault-accounting, then we say the diagrams $D_1$ and $D_2$ are fault-equivalent.
In this section, we'll make this rigorous for the Focked up ZX-calculus, and discuss some fault-equivalent rewrite rules.
Note that while we've used the term `errors' throughout this thesis, Rodatz et al.\ used `faults', and we've stuck with their terminology in the specific phrases `fault-accounting' and `fault-equivalent'.

\subsection{Fault-equivalent diagrams}

We start by giving rigorous definitions of the key concepts.
These are straightforward generalisations of Rodatz et al.'s definition for the qubit case.

\begin{definition}\label{defn:fault_accounting}
    Given two equivalent ZX-diagrams $D_1 = D_2$ with edges $E_1$ and $E_2$ respectively, we say the rewrite $D_1 \mapsto D_2$ is $w$-fault-accounting under a weighted noise model $\FFF$ iff, for all errors $F_2 \in \hat{\DDD}_{|E_2|}$ of weight $\wt(F_2) \leq w$, either this error is detectable, or there is an equivalent error $F_1 \in \hat{\DDD}_{|E_1|}$ with $\wt(F_1) \leq \wt(F_2)$.
    It's fault-accounting iff it's $w$-fault-accounting for all $w \in \RR$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{defn:fault_equivalent_diagrams}
    Two equivalent Focked up ZX-diagrams $D_1 = D_2$ are $w$-fault-equivalent under a weighted noise model $\FFF$ iff the rewrites $D_1 \mapsto D_2$ and $D_2 \mapsto D_1$ are both $w$-fault-accounting.
    They're fault-equivalent iff they're $w$-fault-equivalent for all $w \in \RR$.
\end{definition}

We can then give a simple example and non-example of fault-equivalent diagrams.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:id_rule_fault_equiv}
    The following diagrams are fault-equivalent under the ZX noise model:
    \begin{equation}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/id}
        ~~=~~
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/id_green}
    \end{equation}
    \begin{proof}
        Denote these diagrams as $D_1 = D_2$.
        Note that there are no detectors in either diagram.
        Start with the trivial case of $D_2 \mapsto D_1$.
        Any error $F_1$ on $D_1$ is immediately equivalent to `the same error' $F_2$ in $D_2$:
        \begin{equation}
            \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/id_error}
            ~~=~~
            \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/id_green_error_left}
        \end{equation}
        Certainly here $\wt(F_2) = \wt(F_1)$.
        In the other direction $D_1 \mapsto D_2$, a general error $F_2$ in $D_2$ is equivalent to the following error $F_1$ in $D_1$:
        \begin{equation}
            \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/id_green_error_left_right}
            ~~=~~
            \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/id_error_combined}
        \end{equation}
        In this case, we have:
        \begin{equation}
            \begin{aligned}
                \wt(F_1)
                &= \twonorm{\tbinom{a + c}{b + d} + \tbinom{c}{d}} \\
                &= \twonorm{\tbinom{a}{b} + \tbinom{c}{d}} \\
                &\leq \twonorm{\tbinom{a}{b}} + \twonorm{\tbinom{c}{d}} \\
                &= \onenorm{(\twonorm{\tbinom{a}{b}}, \twonorm{\tbinom{c}{d}})^T)} \\
                &= \wt(F_1)
            \end{aligned}
        \end{equation}
        So all errors in both directions are accounted for, hence the diagrams are fault-equivalent.
    \end{proof}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}
    The following diagrams are not fault-equivalent under the ZX noise model, for any $r \neq 0, 1$:
    \begin{equation}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/identity_equals_multipliers}
    \end{equation}
    \begin{proof}
        Denoting the diagrams as $D_1 = D_2$, the problem is going from $D_1$ to $D_2$.
        This introduces a new internal edge.

        To see this, work backwards; if we start from the diagram $D_2^{F_2}$, note that no matter which way we `push' the error to a boundary edge in order to cancel out the multipliers, its weight will increase:
        \begin{equation}
            \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/red_fault_inside_multipliers_bad}
        \end{equation}
        Similarly if $|r| > 1$ a displacement $F_2 = \hat{D}(\binom{0}{q})$ causes a problem:
        \begin{equation}
            \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/green_fault_inside_multipliers_bad}
        \end{equation}
    \end{proof}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tadpole_rule_fault_equiv}
    The following diagrams are fault-equivalent under the ZX noise model:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:ghz_gets_tadpole}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/ghz_state}
    \quad = \quad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/ghz_state_with_tadpole}
    \end{equation}
    \begin{proof}
        Note there are no detectors in the diagram, so to check for fault-equivalence we just need to check if errors in one diagram are equivalent to errors in the other.
        In one direction there's nothing to prove; all errors $F$ in the left hand side diagram $D_1$ are exactly equivalent to the corresponding errors in the right hand side diagram $D_2$:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:ghz_state_gets_tadpole_boundary_only_faults}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/ghz_state_gets_tadpole_boundary_only_faults}
        \end{equation}
        In the other direction, the equality above also shows that errors that only act non-trivially on the boundary edges of $D_2$ are accounted for in $D_1$.
        So it remains to check errors that act non-trivially on the internal edge of $D_2$ too.
        First consider an error $\hat{D}(\binom{p}{q})$ that acts only on this internal edge; this is equivalent to a $\hat{D}(\binom{0}{q}) = \hat{Z}(q)$ error on any one edge of $D_1$:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:ghz_state_with_tadpole_internal_fault_fine}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/ghz_state_with_tadpole_internal_fault_fine}
        \end{equation}
        Since $wt(\hat{D}(\binom{0}{q})) = \sqrt{q^2} = q$ is less than or equal to $wt(\hat{D}(\binom{p}{q})) = \sqrt{p^2 + q^2}$, this error is no problem.
        We can then use the above equation to show that any error $F_2$ in $D_2$ is accounted for in $D_1$.
        First, we see that $F_2$ is equivalent to an error $F_1$ in $D_1$:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:ghz_state_with_tadpole_any_fault_fine}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/ghz_state_with_tadpole_any_fault_fine}
        \end{equation}
        So we just have to show that $wt(F_1) \leq wt(F_2)$.
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:ghz_state_with_tadpole_any_fault_fine_inequality}
        \begin{aligned}
            wt(F_1) &\leq wt(F_2) \\
            \iff \abs{\sqrt{p_1^2 + (q_1 + q_3)^2}} + \abs{\sqrt{p_2^2 + q_2^2}} &\leq \sum_{j=1}^3 \abs{\sqrt{p_j^2 + q_j^2}} \\
            \iff \abs{\sqrt{p_1^2 + (q_1 + q_3)^2}} &\leq \abs{\sqrt{p_1^2 + q_1^2}} + \abs{\sqrt{p_3^2 + q_3^2}} \\
            \iff \sqrt{p_1^2 + (q_1 + q_3)^2} &\leq \sqrt{p_1^2 + q_1^2} + \sqrt{p_3^2 + q_3^2} \\
        \end{aligned}
        \end{equation}
        To finish, note that by the triangle inequality for the 2-norm we have:
        \begin{equation}
            \begin{aligned}
                \sqrt{p_1^2 + (q_1 + q_3)^2}
                &= \twonorm{\binom{p_1}{q_1} + \binom{0}{q_3}} \\
                &\leq \twonorm{\binom{p_1}{q_1}} + \twonorm{\binom{0}{q_3}} \\
                &= \sqrt{p_1^2 + q_1^2} + \sqrt{q_3^2} \\
                &\leq \sqrt{p_1^2 + q_1^2} + \sqrt{p_3^2 + q_3^2} \\
            \end{aligned}
        \end{equation}
    \end{proof}
    \end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:colour_change_rules_fault_equiv}
    The colour change rules are fault-equivalent rewrites:
    \begin{equation*}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_red}
        ~~=~~
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_green_fouriered}
        \qqquad
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_green}
        ~~=~~
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_red_fouriered}
    \end{equation*}
    \begin{proof}
        We just give the crux of the proof for the first rule (the other is proved identically).
        Denoting the diagrams $D_1 = D_2$, the only potential problem is the introduction of new internal edges going from $D_1 \mapsto D_2$.
        So consider any error $F_2 = \hat{X}(b)\hat{Z}(a)$ on one such internal edge.
        This can be pushed through the (inverse) Fourier transform onto a boundary edge via \Cref{lem:fourier_commutation_rules}.
        At most this may change the error to $\hat{X}(\pm b)\hat{Z}(\pm a)$, which has the same weight:
        \begin{equation*}
            \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/colour_change_internal_error}
            ~~=~~
            \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/colour_change_internal_error_pushed_out}
            ~~=~~
            \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/colour_change_internal_error_pushed_out_cc}
        \end{equation*}
        So now $F_1 = \hat{X}(\pm b)\hat{Z}(\pm a)$ is an equivalent error on $D_1$ with equal weight, so this error $F_2$ is accounted for.
        To complete the proof, one should proceed as we did in \Cref{prop:tadpole_rule_fault_equiv} -- that is, one should consider a general error $F_2$ on $D_2$, use the above to push all errors onto boundary edges, and show the weight of this equivalent error $F_1$ in $D_1$ is less than or equal to that of $F_2$.
    \end{proof}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:multipliers_copy_rule_fault_equiv}
    For $m \in {1, -1}$, the multiplier copy rules of \Cref{lem:multipliers_copy_rule} are fault-equivalent:
    \begin{equation}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_green_multiplier_one_leg_1}
        ~~=~~
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_green_multiplier_one_leg_3}
        \qqquad
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_red_multiplier_one_leg_1}
        ~~=~~
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_red_multiplier_one_leg_3}
    \end{equation}
    \begin{proof}
        First, for $m=1$, the multipliers are the identity, so there's nothing to prove.
        For $m=-1$, the proof is exactly the same as in the previous proposition!
        Because in this case the multipliers are dualisers~\eqref{eq:mult_default_is_dualiser}, so by \Cref{lem:fourier_commutation_rules} a displacement $\hat{X}(b)\hat{Z}(a)$ on an internal error commutes past the dualiser to become $\hat{X}(-b)\hat{Z}(-a)$, which has the same weight.
    \end{proof}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fourier_decomposed_dualiser_fault_equiv}
    The following rules are fault-equivalent rewrites:
    \begin{equation}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/inv_fourier_dualiser_right}
        ~~=~~
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/fourier}
        ~~=~~
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/fault_equivalence/inv_fourier_dualiser_left}
        ~~=~~
    \end{equation}
    \begin{proof}
        Exactly the same as the last two proofs!
        We're just pushing displacement errors past Fourier/inverse Fourier transforms, so weights don't change.
    \end{proof}
\end{proposition}
