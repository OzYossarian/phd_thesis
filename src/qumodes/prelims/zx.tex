\section{The Focked up ZX-calculus}

With the basic algebra of continuous-variable quantum information theory covered, we can switch to a graphical approach via the Focked up ZX-calculus~\cite{shaikh2024fockedupzxcalculuspicturing}.

\subsection{Generators}

As with qubits, one can just think of these ZX-diagrams as being really good notation for linear maps.
Whereas for qubits we started with the green and red spiders, for qumodes we will start with the Fock spider:
\begin{align}\label{eq:fock_spider_defn}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_fock_braces}
    \quad &= \quad
    \sum_{n \in \NN_0} f(n) \ket{n}_F^{\otimes m}\bra{n}_F^{\otimes l}
\end{align}
Here $f$ can be any function $\NN_0 \to \CC$.
Anytime the function label is omitted from a spider, it means the function is the map $x \mapsto 1$.
Throughout, we will use the shorthand $\isarg{x}$ to denote that $x$ is the variable in a single-argument function.
For example, the expression $i^{\isarg{n}}$ is a shorthand for the function $n \mapsto i^{n}$.
Examples of familiar objects defined using the Fock basis are:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fock_spider_examples}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/state_fock} ~~=~~ \ket{m}_F
    \qqquad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/fourier_transform_inverse} ~~=~~ \mathcal{F}
    \qqquad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/fourier_transform} ~~=~~ \mathcal{F}^{-1}
\end{equation}
The Fourier and inverse Fourier transforms can be generalised to a rotation gate:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rotation_gate}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/rotation} ~~=~~ \hat{R}(\theta)
\end{equation}
Next we introduce the green and red spiders:
\begin{align}\label{eq:green_and_fock_spiders_defn}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_green_braces}
    \quad &= \quad
    \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ f(q) \ket{q}_Q^{\otimes m} \bra{q}_Q^{\otimes l} \\
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_red_braces}
    \quad &= \quad
    \int_{\mathbb{R}} dp\ g(p)\ket{p}_P^{\otimes m}\bra{p}_P^{\otimes l}
\end{align}
Here $f$ and $g$ are any functions $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$.
These two spiders are related via Fourier transforms; graphically, this is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:green_red_spiders_related}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_red}
    \quad=\quad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_green_fouriered}
\end{equation}
Letting the function $\chi_a(x) = e^{-i2\pi ax}$, we can write down some familiar objects via the green and red spiders.
First, position and momentum eigenstates:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/state_red_chi}
        &= \int_\mathbb{R} dp\ e^{-i2\pi qp} \ket{p}_P
        = \mathcal{F} \ket{q}_P
        = \ket{q}_Q \\
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/state_green_chi}
        &= \int_\mathbb{R} dq\ e^{i2\pi pq} \ket{q}_Q
        = \mathcal{F}^{-1} \ket{p}_Q
        = \ket{p}_P
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Next, displacements, via the spectral theorem of \Cref{eq:position_momentum_spectral_theorem}:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/gate_red_chi}
        &= \int_\mathbb{R} dp\ e^{-i2\pi qp} \ket{p}_P \bra{p}_P
        = e^{-i2\pi q\hat{p}}
        = \hat{X}(q) \\
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/gate_green_chi}
        &= \int_\mathbb{R} dq\ e^{i2\pi pq} \ket{q}_Q \bra{q}_Q
        = e^{i2\pi p\hat{q}}
        = \hat{Z}(p)
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Importantly, unlike in the qubit case, the green cup is not equal to the red cup:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qubits/prelims/zx/state_bell_green}
    ~~\neq~~
    \tikzfig{qubits/prelims/zx/state_bell_red}
\end{equation}
So whereas in the qubit case we could define the `spiderless' cup to be the green or red cup without any problems, we can't do this for qumodes.
The most common solution here is just to define the spiderless cup to be the green cup.
We will essentially take this approach -- we'll forbid cups and caps from being explicitly spiderless, but update any definitions that relied on spiderless cups and caps to use green ones.
For example, we said in the qubit case the transpose of a diagram was formed by bending outputs to inputs and vice versa using spiderless cups and caps.
In the Focked up case, we'll use green cups and caps:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/diagram}
    ~~\xmapsto{(-)^T}~~
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/diagram_transposed}
    ~~\eqqcolon~~
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/diagram_transposed_straightened}
\end{equation}

A consequence of this is that we lose the very desirable property that only connectivity matters!
Specifically, the cost of changing the direction of an edge between a red and green spider is the addition of a Fock gate labelled $(-1)^{\isarg{n}}$ (sometimes called a \textit{dualiser}) to that edge:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spider_green_red_change_order_adds_dualiser}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_green_red_change_order_short}
\end{equation}
For example, we can no longer draw (the CV analogue of) the CNOT gate with a vertical edge, because it's ambiguous which of two unequal diagrams this would mean:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/cnot_green_first}
    \quad \neq \quad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/cnot_red_first}
\end{equation}
We must therefore make clear whether an edge is an input or output of a given spider.
This could be indicated by making edges directed, which restores only connectivity matters, but I personally find this even more confusing:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/cnot_green_first_arrow}
    \quad \coloneqq \quad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/cnot_red_first}
\end{equation}
So instead we'll define that edges incident to the leftmost half of a generator are strictly inputs, and edges incident to the rightmost half of a generator are strictly outputs.
The final non-trivial generator we'll need is the multiplier:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier_defn}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/multiplier}
    \quad = \quad
    \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ \ket{mq}_Q \bra{q}_Q
\end{equation}
This can be expressed in terms of existing generators:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/multiplier}
    \quad = \quad
    \begin{cases}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/multiplier_decomposed_m} &\text{ if $m \neq 0$} \\[5pt]
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/multiplier_decomposed_0_padded} &\text{ if $m = 0$}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The multiplier appears in the diagrams for the $\CX(s)$ and $\CZ(s)$ gates:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/cxs} ~~=~~ \CX(s)
    \qqqquad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/czs} ~~=~~ \CZ(s)
\end{equation}
When $s=1$, it can be checked that the multiplier is just the identity, so we get
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/cx} ~~=~~ \CX
    \qqqquad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/cz} ~~=~~ \CZ
\end{equation}

Conjugation of a diagram corresponds to conjugating all labels, transposition (as we've seen) corresponds to reflection across a vertical line, and taking the adjoint means performing both of these operations.
We also classify the identity and SWAP maps as generators.

\subsection{Combining diagrams}

We can again compose diagrams vertically (tensor product) and horizontally (composition of operators).
But it's worth discussing briefly what operator composition in particular means, given that we'll end up with expressions with continuous functionals in them.
For example, expressions that might reasonably cause us a headache are those of the form:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/gate_fg_green}
        ~~&= \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ g(q) \ket{q}_Q \bra{q}_Q \circ \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq'\ f(q') \ket{q'}_Q \bra{q'}_Q \\
        &= \int_{\mathbb{R}} \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ dq' g(q) f(q') \ket{q}_Q \prepostsub{Q}{\braket{q|q'}}{Q} \bra{q'}_Q \\
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
So here we have a functional $\prepostsub{Q}{\braket{q|q'}}{Q} = \delta_{q - q'}$ under an integral -- it's not at all clear what this could mean!
Annoyingly, the interpretation here is that the integral is no longer an actual integral, but rather very involved notation for functional application.
That is, for functional $F$, the expression $\int dx f(x) F$ is just alternative notation for $F(f)$.
So in the scenario above we can interpret this expression as:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        &\phantom{=} \int_{\mathbb{R}} \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ dq' g(q) f(q') \ket{q}_Q \prepostsub{Q}{\braket{q|q'}}{Q} \bra{q'}_Q \\
        &= \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ g(q) \ket{q}_Q  \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq'\ f(q') \prepostsub{Q}{\braket{q|q'}}{Q} \bra{q'}_Q \\
        &= \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ g(q) \ket{q}_Q  \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq'\ f(q') \bra{q'}_Q \delta_{q - q'}  \\
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus in the expression $\int_{\mathbb{R}} dq'\ f(q') \bra{q'}_Q \delta_{q - q'}$ we're really saying apply the functional $\delta_{q - q'}$ to the function $f(q') \bra{q'}_Q$.
But this is still ill-defined, because $f(q') \bra{q'}_Q$ isn't a Schwartz function -- it's not even a function.
So the application of $\delta_{q - q'}$ to this (evaluation at $q' = q$) is purely formal, giving the result $f(q) \bra{q}_Q$.
Therefore the final expression is:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        &\phantom{=} \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ g(q) \ket{q}_Q  \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq'\ f(q') \bra{q'}_Q \delta_{q - q'} \\
        &= \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ g(q) \ket{q}_Q  f(q) \bra{q}_Q \\
        &= \int_{\mathbb{R}} dq\ g(q) f(q) \ket{q}_Q \bra{q}_Q
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We'll see in a second that this does indeed match up with what we'd get by graphically simplifying the expression:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/gate_fg_green}
    ~~=~~
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/gate_fg_green_fused}
\end{equation}

An important special case of this rule is the commutation relation between displacement operators $\hat{X}(a)$ and $\hat{Z}(b)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:displacement_commutation_relation_zx}
    \begin{equation}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/pauli_webs/displacements_commute_red_green}
    \end{equation}
    \begin{equation}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/focked_up/pauli_webs/displacements_commute_green_red}
    \end{equation}
    \begin{proof}
        The first follows from the first equation of \Cref{lem:gate_copy_blank_spider}, with $f(x) = \overline{\chi}_a(x) = e^{i2\pi ax}$.
        So:
        \begin{equation}
            f(x + b) = e^{i2\pi a(x + b)} = e^{i2\pi ab}e^{i2\pi ax} = e^{i2\pi ab} \overline{\chi}_a(x)
        \end{equation}
        where the scalar $e^{i2\pi ab}$ can be pulled out of the spider to become a global phase.
        The second follows from the second equation of \Cref{lem:gate_copy_blank_spider}, with $f(x) = \chi_b(x) = e^{-i2\pi bx}$.
        So:
        \begin{equation}
            f(x + a) = e^{-i2\pi b(x + a)} = e^{-i2\pi ab}e^{-i2\pi bx} = e^{-i2\pi ab} \chi_b(x)
        \end{equation}
    \end{proof}
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The Gaussian + combs fragment}

We said earlier the CV analogue of Clifford unitaries are the Gaussian unitaries.
The CV analogue of the Clifford fragment of the qubit ZX-calculus is the Gaussian fragment.
This is exactly the space of diagrams generated by green and red spiders whose function labels are quadratics, plus the vacuum state.
That is, it's all diagrams generated by:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_green_quadratic}
    \qqqquad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_red_quadratic}
    \qqqquad
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/state_vacuum}
\end{equation}
We will in fact be interested in adding one more type of spider to the mix.
For any predicate $f: X \to \BB$, i.e.\ any function that returns true or false, let $\indicator{f(x)}$ be the function that's $1$ if $f(x)$ is true and $0$ if not.
Then also let $x \eqmod{r} c$ mean $x = c \text{ mod } r$.
So:
\begin{equation}
   \indicator{x \eqmod{r} c} =
   \begin{cases}
       1 &\text{ if $x = c \text{ mod } r$} \\
       0 &\text{ else}
   \end{cases}
\end{equation}
We call spiders with these labels \textit{comb spiders}, per the discussion earlier around \Cref{eq:dirac_comb_as_real_function}:
\begin{align}\label{eq:green_and_red_comb_spiders}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_green_comb}
    \quad &= \quad
    \sum_{k \in \ZZ} \ket{c + kr}_Q^{\otimes m} \bra{c + kr}_Q^{\otimes l} \\
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/spider_red_comb}
    \quad &= \quad
    \sum_{k \in \ZZ} \ket{c + kr}_P^{\otimes m} \bra{c + kr}_P^{\otimes l}
\end{align}

In particular, we can represent codestates of GKP codes as comb spiders.
For example, in \Cref{eq:single_mode_square_GKP_computational_codestates} we determined the codestates $\ket{0}_L$ and $\ket{1}_L$ of the square single-mode GKP code, which we will henceforth denote with a square subscript:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single_mode_square_GKP_computational_codestates_square_subscript}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/state_GKP_square_green_0_indicator}
        &~~=~ \sum_{k \in \ZZ} \ket{\sqrt{2}k}_Q
        = \sum_{k \in \ZZ} \delta_{\sqrt{2}k}
        = \ket{0_\square} \\
        \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/state_GKP_square_green_1_indicator}
        &~~=~ \sum_{k \in \ZZ} \ket{1/\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{2}k}_Q
        = \sum_{k \in \ZZ} \delta_{1/\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{2}k}
        = \ket{1_\square}
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
As a result, we'll in future use $0_\square$ as a shorthand for the function $\indicator{x \eqmod{\sqrt{2}} 0}$, and $1_\square$ for $\indicator{x \eqmod{\sqrt{2}} 1/\sqrt{2}}$.

\subsection{Homodyne and GKP measurements}\label{subsec:homodyne_and_gkp_measurements}

In \Cref{subsec:qubit_pauli_measurements} we showed how to represent a Pauli measurement with outcome $m \in \{-1, 1\}$ in the ZX-calculus.
The analogous case we'll need here is that of homodyne and GKP measurements,
These will turn out to look almost identical, which shouldn't be so surprising, given that we said in \Cref{subsec:gkp} that GKP measurements are just homodyne measurements mod 1.
Once again, the diagrams we'll really draw don't represent the measurements themselves, but rather the result of getting outcome $a$ in such a measurement.
In other words, for a measurement consisting of operators $\{M_a\}$, we'll give the diagram for $M_a$.

One can derive this diagram in the same way as we did for the qubit case in \Cref{subsec:qubit_pauli_measurements}, where we performed some ZX-rewrites until we had decomposed our diagram into a sum of Pauli operators.
Alternatively, we can be lazy and just find circuits for performing homodyne and GKP measurements in the literature and translate them into ZX-diagrams.
Either way, given $m$ qumodes, let $\hat{D}(\tbinom{\bsym{v}}{\bsym{u}}) = e^{-i2\pi \hat{\bsym{r}}(\tbinom{\boldsymbol{v}}{\boldsymbol{u}})}$ be a displacement operator, with $\hat{\bsym{r}}(\tbinom{\boldsymbol{v}}{\boldsymbol{u}})$ the corresponding quadrature operator.
As in the qubit case, the `core' of the diagram will be the measurement of the GKP stabilizer/quadrature operator in the case that $\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}(\tbinom{\boldsymbol{v}}{\boldsymbol{u}}) = \hat{p}_1 + \ldots + \hat{p}_n$, i.e.\ the case $\tbinom{\boldsymbol{v}}{\boldsymbol{u}} = \tbinom{\boldsymbol{1}}{\boldsymbol{0}}$.
For the homodyne measurement, the diagram for this is:
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/meas_homodyne_all_z}
\end{equation}
To convert this into a GKP measurement, we intuitively just have to add the `mod 1' part.
We do this by adding a comb function to the `measurement spider':
\begin{equation}
    \tikzfig{qumodes/prelims/zx/meas_gkp_all_z}
\end{equation}
As a sanity check, we can note that $\indicator{x \eqmod{1} 0} \cdot \chi_{a + k} = \indicator{x \eqmod{1} 0} \cdot \chi_{a}$ for all $k \in \ZZ$, hence our measurement outcome $a$ really is only given mod 1.
Then for more general measurements, we can convert each pair of phase space coordinates $(v_j, u_j)$ to polar form $r_j e^{i\theta_j}$, and rotate/stretch phase space on each qumode accordingly, so that instead of measuring $\hat{p}_1 + \ldots + \hat{p}_n = \hat{\boldsymbol{r}}(\tbinom{\boldsymbol{1}}{\boldsymbol{0}})$ we're measuring $\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}(\tbinom{\boldsymbol{v}}{\boldsymbol{u}})$.
For the homodyne and GKP measurements respectively, this looks like:
